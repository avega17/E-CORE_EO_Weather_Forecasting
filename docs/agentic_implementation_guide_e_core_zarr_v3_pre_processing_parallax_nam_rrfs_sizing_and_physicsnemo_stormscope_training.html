1 · Single-Instance Plain Zarr v3

The virtualized catalog (native.icechunk) was designed to materialize once per allocation into node-local scratch, never on the fly per epoch. Because the materialized dataset uses neither virtualization nor multi-branch versioning—and Icechunk adds a 4.2× read overhead—we standardize exclusively on plain Zarr v3 (data.zarr, Zstd).

2 · Stock XarrayDataset + ProcessedSource

Writing the materialized store as plain Zarr v3 makes PhysicsNeMo's built-in xarray.XarrayDataset the clear winner for training and fine-tuning—eliminating custom PhysicsNeMo dataset classes while retaining ProcessedSource strictly as our Earth2Studio DataSource for inference.

3 · Direct PhysicsNeMo Fine-Tuning

We drop PyTorch Lightning wrappers (which conflict with Earth2Studio's @torch.inference_mode() ODE sampler and PhysicsNeMo's ShardTensor mesh) and train or fine-tune StormScope and StormScope Flash directly via PhysicsNeMo's regional_weather_diffusion/train.py.

Standardizing on Single-Instance Plain Zarr v3 Over Icechunk Eliminates I/O Overhead and Dual-Format Maintenance

Clarifying the role of preparation/ vs. processed/ in src/ecore_weather/processing/ makes the storage backend choice unambiguous: plain Zarr v3 with lossless Blosc/Zstd compression is faster, simpler, and natively supported by PhysicsNeMo.

In our current architecture (jobs/polaris_process.pbs and docs/dataset_processing.md), the virtual catalog in preparation/<selection_id>/ was never meant to be dynamically materialized on the fly every training epoch; rather, it was designed to materialize a transformed copy once per job allocation into Polaris node-local NVMe scratch (/local/scratch/.../processed) or onto a single dedicated directory on Workstation DAS (/mnt/p/ecore_eo_datasets/processed). Now that our workflow explicitly shifts to accepting a single-instance-at-a-time non-virtualized copy of the dataset with pre-processing applied, maintaining support for both Icechunk and Zarr v3 in process_store.py is unnecessary technical debt.

Evaluating Icechunk against plain Zarr v3 for this single-instance materialized store points decisively to plain Zarr v3 (data.zarr) for four reasons verified in our codebase and upstream documentation:

  • 4.2× Higher Measured Window Read Throughput: In our controlled October 27, 2026 storage benchmark (docs/dataset_processing.md and process_benchmarks.py), plain Zarr v3 directories with Zstd compression delivered 2.65 six-frame windows/s (with 6-frame chunks and 4 readers) and 1.83 twelve-frame windows/s (12-frame chunks), whereas ProcessedSource reading from data.icechunk achieved only 0.575–0.629 six-frame windows/s (1–2 readers, with no gain at 4 readers).
  • No Use of Virtualization or Branching on the Derived Store: Once pixels are calibrated, parallax-corrected, and interpolated onto the common Lambert Conformal Conic (LCC) grid, every chunk is a newly computed physical float32 array. Resumable batch integrity is already enforced by our own progress.json (which records per-batch SHA-256 digests and verified_slots), owner.lock, and complete.json. Icechunk's snapshot transaction layer duplicates checks we already perform while adding cold-start and metadata lookup overhead.
  • Direct Compatibility with Stock PhysicsNeMo XarrayDataset and Disk Caching: PhysicsNeMo's xarray.XarrayDataset opens standard Zarr paths via xarray.open_dataset(path, engine="zarr") and provides a built-in node-local disk cache (cache_location). An Icechunk repository requires opening a Python icechunk.Repository.readonly_session().store object before calling Xarray, which cannot be passed as a plain string path in Hydra YAML.
  • Multi-Worker PyTorch fork Safety: Standard PyTorch DataLoader workers are spawned via fork. Plain Zarr v3 filesystem stores read independent chunk files without a background Rust Tokio async runtime, avoiding multi-process lock contention across DataLoader workers.

GOES Cloud-Top Parallax Correction Over Puerto Rico: Physics, Trusted Satpy/Pyresample Implementation, and Independent ACHA Acquisition Task

The StormScope paper (Pathak et al., 2026, Section 3) explicitly notes that its baseline omits parallax correction and flags steep viewing angles as a primary limitation for future work. Over Puerto Rico and the Caribbean, correcting parallax is essential when pairing geostationary satellite channels (`ABI-L2-CMIPF`) with ground-based radar (`MRMS` / `TJUA`) at 1–3 km scales.

1 · Physical Mechanism & Magnitude Over Puerto Rico

GOES-East (`GOES-16` prior to April 7, 2025 at 15:00 UTC; `GOES-19` thereafter) orbits above the equator at $75.2^\circ\text{W}$ at an altitude of $H_{\text{sat}} \approx 35{,}786\text{ km}$. Because Puerto Rico lies off-nadir around $18.2^\circ\text{N}, 66.4^\circ\text{W}$, the satellite views the island at a local zenith angle of $\theta_z \approx 23^\circ\text{–}25^\circ$. When a deep tropical thunderstorm or hurricane eyewall reaches an altitude of $h = 14\text{–}17\text{ km}$ above the WGS84 ellipsoid, the satellite's oblique line of sight projects the cold cloud top northeastward (radially away from $0^\circ\text{N}, 75.2^\circ\text{W}$) by $\Delta d \approx h \tan(\theta_z) \approx 6.5\text{–}7.5\text{ km}$ relative to its true surface rain core.

On our target LCC grids, a $6.7\text{ km}$ displacement shifts convective cloud tops by 2.2 pixels at 3 km, 3.3 pixels at 2 km, and 6.7 pixels at 1 km. Over an island only $65\text{ km}$ wide north-to-south with steep orographic rain bands over the Cordillera Central and El Yunque, uncorrected parallax misaligns `C13` infrared cooling from `MRMS` radar cores and coastline boundaries.

2 · Why Our Satpy & Pyresample Code Can Be Trusted

Our implementation in src/ecore_weather/processing/process_parallax.py (`mapping_from_height()`) leverages two mature, peer-reviewed open-source libraries—Satpy (`satpy.modifiers.parallax.ParallaxCorrection`, v0.59.0) and Pyresample (`pyresample.bilinear.NumpyBilinearResampler`, v1.35.0)—augmented by three strict scientific safeguards:

  • Exact Orbital Ellipsoid Intersection: `ParallaxCorrection(base_area).corrected_area(height)` reads the scan's actual `goes_imager_projection` orbital parameters (`satellite_actual_longitude`, `satellite_actual_latitude`, `satellite_actual_altitude`) and solves the 3D vector line-of-sight intersection with the WGS84 ellipsoid at height `HT`, matching the operational formulation validated by Bernal Ayala et al. (2023).
  • Independent Unknown-Height Mask (`known`): Satpy internally replaces `NaN` cloud heights with `0 m` so clear-sky pixels remain unshifted. However, if a cloudy pixel has a corrupt/invalid height retrieval (`DQF != 0`), treating it as `0 m` would create a false tear. Lines 48–50 of `process_parallax.py` compute `known = resample_nearest(harea, valid, base, radius_of_influence=15000)` to mask uncertain cloud-height boundaries explicitly.
  • Single-Pass Bilinear Resampling: Rather than interpolating native pixels onto a shifted geostationary grid and interpolating a second time onto the LCC grid (which compounds smoothing error), `mapping_from_height()` wraps the shifted `(lon, lat)` coordinates in a `SwathDefinition` and computes 4-neighbor bilinear weights directly onto the target LCC grid in one pass.

Currently, `process_parallax.py:fetch_height()` fetches individual `ABI-L2-ACHAF` files on demand from `s3://noaa-goes16` / `s3://noaa-goes19` into a 2 GiB NetCDF cache solely for the 4-frame `parallax_pilot` (`C02` and `C13`). Materializing a full parallax-corrected Zarr v3 store requires an independent acquisition task to be specced and executed by the agent:

  • Preserved Variables & Layout: Crop to `PR_BBOX` on the native `ACHAF` fixed grid (2 km at nadir for modern Enterprise ACHA; ~80–100 KB per cropped scan, or ~25–35 GB total compressed Zarr ZIPs across all 66 months), preserving `HT` (packed/float cloud-top height in meters), `DQF` (quality flags), `x`, `y`, `goes_imager_projection`, and scan `_s`/`_e` times inside monthly `goes/ABI-L2-ACHAF/{goes16|goes19}/roi-///raw.zarr.zip` archives.

Unified Zarr v3 Schema for Earth2Studio Inference (ProcessedSource) and PhysicsNeMo Training (Stock xarray.XarrayDataset)

By materializing to a single plain Zarr v3 store (`data.zarr`), **Pathway A (`xarray.XarrayDataset` in `datasets/xarray.py` of PhysicsNeMo `regional_weather_diffusion`) is the clear winner for training and fine-tuning**. It eliminates the need to write or maintain a custom `CustomDataset` subclass in PhysicsNeMo while supporting multi-threaded Zarr reads (`num_threads`), node-local NVMe chunk caching (`cache_location`), automatic `cos_zenith` computation (`solar_angles`), and `nan_mask: true` token masking.

As documented in PhysicsNeMo's Xarray Dataset specification, `XarrayDataset` requires: (1) a `time` coordinate (`datetime64[ns]`), (2) data arrays with dimensions `(time, channel, y, x)` where the channel dimension has string coordinates with the variable names, (3) an optional 1D `valid(time)` boolean array marking complete eligible timestamps so missing slots are skipped without rewriting files, and (4) a separate `invariants.zarr` file with `(y, x)` arrays (`latitude`, `longitude`, `inside_bbox`, and optional `topography`) plus a `stats.json` normalization file.

Remarkably, our existing `src/ecore_weather/processing/process_store.py` **already writes `values` with dimensions `(time, variable, y, x)` where invalid pixels are set to `np.nan`!** Making `process_store.py` write directly to plain Zarr v3 (`data.zarr`) and adding the 1D `valid(time)` boolean array (true where all variables in `recipe.variables` have a matched observation at that slot) makes our materialized store 100% compatible with both `ProcessedSource` (for Earth2Studio inference) and stock `xarray.XarrayDataset` (for PhysicsNeMo training):

class ProcessedSource(DataSource):
    """Physical-unit Earth2Studio DataSource reading materialized plain Zarr v3 store."""
    def __init__(self, path, allow_partial=False, cache_metadata=True):
        self.path = Path(path)
        self.cache_metadata = cache_metadata
        self._dataset = None
        self._open_lock = threading.Lock()
        if not allow_partial and not (self.path / "complete.json").is_file():
            raise ValueError("A completed materialized store is required")
        self.state = json.loads((self.path / "progress.json").read_text())

    def _open_zarr(self):
        ds = xr.open_zarr(str(self.path / "data.zarr"), consolidated=False, decode_times=True, mask_and_scale=False, chunks=None)
        ds = ds.isel(time=slice(0, self.state["verified_slots"]))
        ds.latitude.load(); ds.longitude.load()
        return ds

Note one subtle naming detail in `XarrayDataset`: as documented in PhysicsNeMo's `XARRAY_DATASET.html`, `XarrayDataset` checks for an optional 1D boolean variable named **`valid` with dimension `(time,)`** to filter out missing timestamps, whereas our existing `process_store.py` named the 4D pixel validity mask `valid(time, variable, y, x)`. Renaming our 4D pixel validity array to `pixel_valid(time, variable, y, x)` (since `values` already encodes invalid pixels as `NaN` for `nan_mask: true`) and reserving `valid(time)` for the 1D timestamp eligibility mask makes `data.zarr` open cleanly in stock `xarray.XarrayDataset` with zero naming collisions.

Training and Fine-Tuning StormScope & StormScope Flash Directly in PhysicsNeMo

We omit PyTorch Lightning wrappers from our implementation plan: Earth2Studio's StormScopeGOES and StormScopeMRMS classes are inference-only ODE rollout iterators running under @torch.inference_mode(), and wrapping PhysicsNeMo's inner DiT in Lightning breaks ShardTensor domain parallelism and native .mdlus checkpointing. Instead, all regional training and fine-tuning execute directly through PhysicsNeMo's examples/weather/regional_weather_diffusion/train.py.

1 · Checkpoint Architecture & Experts

In nvidia/stormscope-goes-mrms, the 3 km / 10-minute model (260M parameters) and its distilled StormScope Flash variant use a Diffusion Transformer (`model.architecture: dit`) with 2D Neighborhood Attention (`patch_size: [4, 4]`, `hidden_size: 768`, `depth: 16`, `num_heads: 12`, `attn_kernel_size: [31, 31]`, `attention_backend: "natten2d_rope"`). Because `natten2d_rope` uses 2D Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) rather than fixed absolute grid embeddings, the exact same `.mdlus` weights can be loaded and fine-tuned on our `288×280` (3 km) or `256×256` cropped Caribbean grid via `training.initial_weights`.

2 · Fine-Tuning Teacher vs. Flash Experts

Baseline StormScope partitions $\sigma \in [\sigma_{\min}, \sigma_{\max}]$ into two EDM denoiser experts (`coarse` and `fine`), whereas StormScope Flash distills the trajectory into three noise-regime experts (`expert_0.mdlus` for low $\sigma$, `expert_1.mdlus` for middle $\sigma$, and `expert_2.mdlus` for high $\sigma$ across `0.001–800.0` for GOES and `0.005–800.0` for MRMS/GLM), requiring only 5 GOES and 7 MRMS evaluations per 10-minute step. In `regional_weather_diffusion/train.py`, each expert `.mdlus` archive is a standard `EDMPreconditioner` checkpoint that can be fine-tuned independently on its designated $[\sigma_{\min}, \sigma_{\max}]$ interval.

3 · Key Stability & Masking Flags on Polaris

When launching `torchrun --standalone --nproc_per_node=4 train.py` on ALCF Polaris (4× A100 40 GB GPUs), three Hydra settings in `reference_stormscope.yaml` are mandatory: (1) `model.hyperparameters.use_nan_mask_tokens: true` (pools invalid pixels to 4×4 tokens, substitutes learned mask tokens inside NATTEN, and zeroes their loss contribution), (2) `model.hyperparameters.attn_kwargs.qk_norm: true` (prevents DiT attention logit blowup under `amp-bf16`), and (3) `training.loss.track_sigma_bin_loss: true` (logs 8 equal-probability noise bins to verify convergence across coarse and fine regimes).

Operational regional convection-allowing NWP coverage across our entire 5.5-year study period (`2021-01-01` to `2026-07-01`)—and up to its permanent retirement on **October 14, 2026**—is provided by the **NAM Puerto Rico Nest (`priconest`, NCEP Grid 194)** in `s3://noaa-nam-pds/nam.YYYYMMDD/nam.tCCz.priconest.hiresfFF.tm00.grib2` (with companion `.idx` byte-range index files):
